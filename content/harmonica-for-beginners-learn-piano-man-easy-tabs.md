## The anatomy of the harmonica

*[Watch from 0:47](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=h_JR-aGZU2E&t=47s)*

My harmonica is a Hohner Special 20 in the key of C. Make sure yours says the letter C on it, because that's the key it's in. C stands for C major, but you can get a harmonica in all 12 keys. If yours is an A or an E or something else, you can still learn to play it, but the pitches won't match up with mine. Don't get rid of it, though, because someday you might play a song in that key.

The harmonica is a very basic instrument. The main piece inside is called the comb, the top two metal pieces are the covers, and inside are two reed plates. The reeds look like little diving boards or tongues. You can take your harmonica apart, but I don't recommend it unless one of the reeds is stuck. To clean an old harmonica, I run it under tap water in the sink and then tap it out to get all the moisture out. Mine probably needs cleaning because it's about 15 years old.

## How to hold the harmonica

*[Watch from 1:49](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=h_JR-aGZU2E&t=109s)*

We use our left hand. Keep your fingers flat, then bring your thumb out parallel with a little space between it and your fingers. Slide the harmonica into that space and squeeze down on it. Make sure the first hole on the left is one and the last hole on the right is 10. You should be able to see the numbers written on top. Also make sure you're not blocking the holes on the back with your hand, so the air and the sound can escape.

You only need your left hand to play, and in this lesson we'll just use our left hands. Later on I'll show you right hand technique, which is kind of fun.

## Blowing and drawing

*[Watch from 2:42](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=h_JR-aGZU2E&t=162s)*

We can basically blow or draw air. When you blow, you're breathing out, and when you draw, you're breathing in. I'm playing holes one, two and three here.

When you blow, make sure your lips are curling around the harmonica and no air is leaking out around the corners of your mouth. Keep your lips relaxed and not too firm, for now. Eventually you'll need to learn single notes, and then you'll need a firm embouchure.

You need a space between your teeth. It might help to say the syllable "ah" before you play, just to get the right shape. Don't let your teeth touch the harmonica. It's almost like your lips are wrapping around it. I know that sounds weird.

## Breathing from the diaphragm

*[Watch from 4:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=h_JR-aGZU2E&t=253s)*

To breathe properly, we need to breathe from our diaphragm, like a singer would. If you're a singer, or you've done yoga or other deep breathing exercises, you probably know how. Breathe deeply from your belly. If you breathe in deeply with support, you should feel your stomach puff out. If you breathe up high in your chest, you'll get shallow, unsupported breaths. The best way to breathe is low, using your diaphragm to support your lungs.

## Reading harmonica tabs

*[Watch from 4:57](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=h_JR-aGZU2E&t=297s)*

Harmonica tabs are the system we use for learning songs. It's a numbered system, and each number refers to a hole on the harmonica. If we see 1 2 3, we blow into holes 1, 2 and 3. If we see a minus in front of 1 2 3, we draw air in through those holes.

Something confusing is that we can't see which holes we're blowing into as we play, so we have to use our ears and trust that we're playing the right notes. Right now we're playing chords, which means several holes at a time. There's a little wiggle room, because sometimes you can play an extra hole and it still sounds good. Until we get to single notes, we shouldn't worry too much about 100% accuracy. Just play it and have fun.

Let's try a tab: 1 2 3, minus 1 2 3, 1 2 3. That's blow, draw, blow on one, two, three. Then try it a little faster. Next, go to holes four, five and six. Then play 456, minus 456, 456 and a rest. A rest just means silence. Finally, put them together: 1 2 3, minus 1 2 3, 1 2 3, then 456, minus 456, 456.

## Playing along with a jam track

*[Watch from 7:37](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=h_JR-aGZU2E&t=457s)*

Now we play along with a jam track. Just do your best to play the right notes.

You may have struggled with your breath there, and that's okay. Timing your breaths takes practice. You may also have found it difficult to articulate, meaning to play the same thing over and over. What can really help is changing how you start a note. Use your tongue to start notes, kind of like saying the syllable "du du du du", except you don't actually say it.

It's the same with drawing air in. We use our tongue to stop the air and build pressure, and that way we get nice strong notes.

## Learning Piano Man

*[Watch from 9:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=h_JR-aGZU2E&t=590s)*

Now let's learn Piano Man by Billy Joel. Why didn't they call it harmonica man? "Sing us a song, you're the harmonica man." Okay, singing the word harmonica isn't easy.

We'll be playing two-note chords, so instead of 456 we're just playing 56. If you're unsure which holes you're playing, try a little trick: cover all the holes you're not supposed to play with your fingers, everything but five and six, and put that up to your mouth to test whether you're playing the right notes.

The intro has four lines of music.

- **Line one:** 56, minus 56, 56, minus 45, 45, minus 454, 5.
- **Line two:** 34, minus 34, 34, 45, minus 34, 45, minus 45. The 45 and minus 45 on the end are like a lead-in to the third line, so they're just hanging on the end.
- **Line three:** the same as the first line, 56, minus 56, 56, then minus 45, 4 5, minus 454, 5.
- **Line four:** 34, minus 45, 45, minus 34, 34.

It helps to wet your lips a little before you play so you can slide the harmonica freely. Otherwise it kind of gets stuck if it's too dry.

## Fake it until you make it

*[Watch from 13:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=h_JR-aGZU2E&t=783s)*

When you're first learning the harmonica, I recommend you fake it until you make it. You might not know if you're playing the right notes at first, so just play with confidence and have fun with it. I mean, it's a harmonica, for crying out loud. Now let's try all of that together, slowly.
