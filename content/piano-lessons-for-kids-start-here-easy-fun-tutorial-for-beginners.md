*Adapted from the video [Piano Lessons for Kids: START HERE - Easy & Fun Tutorial for Beginners](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wjnkzXmiTgE)*

> **TL;DR:** Every key on the keyboard has a letter, and the most important one to find first is middle C. You can find it by looking at the pattern of two and three black keys: C sits just to the left of the two black keys in the middle. I label C D E F G with a dry erase marker, then we practice playing them up and down. We finish with seesaw, my first-lesson exercise, with the right thumb on C and one finger on each of the five notes.

Hello everyone, and welcome to this piano lesson for kids. Today we're going to talk about how to find letters on the keyboard.

The most important thing to understand when starting out is that each key has a letter. A full keyboard has 88 keys, though you might have a smaller one. How many keys does your keyboard have?

I made a little printout that I set behind the keys for my students, and it shows the letter for each key.

## Finding middle C

*[Watch from 0:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wjnkzXmiTgE&t=35s)*

The most important key to find is middle C, right here in the middle. You find it by looking at the black keys. There's a pattern: two, three, two, three, two, three. Find two black keys in the middle, and right to the left of them is C.

I take a dry erase marker and write C on that key. Make sure it's a dry erase marker, because you want it to come off. Permanent marker would stay on the key, and that's no good. A pencil works too, since it will erase.

## Labeling C D E F G

*[Watch from 1:28](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wjnkzXmiTgE&t=88s)*

According to my handy printout, our letters are C, D, E, F and G. It keeps going, but that's all we need for now, so write C D E F G on your keys.

Now that we know where middle C is, go ahead and play it. If it sounds too low, that's still a C, just a lower one. If it sounds too high, it's also a C. We want middle C right here. You can use your right or left hand and any finger you like. I'm using my pointer finger.

These letters are part of our regular alphabet, but starting on C. If we skipped A and B, then C right here begins our alphabet: A B C D E F G.

## Practicing the letters

*[Watch from 2:33](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wjnkzXmiTgE&t=153s)*

Let's practice finding some letters.

- **C, D, E.** Play them, then try again.
- **E, D, C.** Go back down, and then once more.
- **C D E F G.** Play all the labeled keys in a row, then repeat.
- **G F E D C.** Go back down the same way, and then repeat it together.

Very good.

## Seesaw

*[Watch from 4:14](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wjnkzXmiTgE&t=254s)*

Here's an exercise I like to teach in my very first lesson for my piano students. I call it seesaw.

We use the right hand. Align your hand on the keys so your thumb is on C and each of your five fingers can play one of the five notes. Seesaw goes C, D, E, F, G, then G, F, E, D, C, using each finger. Can you try that?
